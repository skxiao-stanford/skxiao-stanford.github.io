skxiao@stanford.edu · CV · Google Scholar

Research

Cells and organs talk to each other with small molecules. My work finds those molecules and works out the enzymes, transporters, and receptors that control their synthesis, transport, and signaling.

Signaling metabolites in metabolism and pharmacology

I showed that the exercise-inducible metabolite Lac-Phe mediates the effects of metformin on food intake and body weight, and identified MCT6 as the intestinal exporter required for that effect — a coupled enzyme–transporter axis for N-lactoyl amino acids in energy homeostasis.

Chemical messengers from extreme physiology

Animals with extreme physiology solve metabolic problems we cannot. Using python metabolomics, I uncovered a conserved postprandial metabolite that acts through a gut–brain pathway to suppress feeding, and I am extending this approach to hibernating bears.

Chemoreception and the environment

During my PhD with John Carlson at Yale, I studied how Drosophila detects chemical cues — including robust olfactory responses in the absence of odorant binding proteins, and taste avoidance of metal ions.
